1. Fundamental Design ParadigmsBrute Force: Exhaustively evaluates every potential candidate in the search space until a valid solution is found. It guarantees finding an optimal solution if one exists, but scales poorly (often $O(n!)$ or $O(2^n)$), making it impractical for large input sizes.Divide and Conquer: Recursively breaks down a problem into two or more independent subproblems of the same or related type, until they become simple enough to be solved directly. The solutions to the subproblems are then combined to solve the original problem.Example: Merge Sort splits an unsorted array of size $n$ into halves, sorts them recursively in $O(\log n)$ levels, and merges them in $O(n)$ time per level, producing an overall time complexity of $O(n \log n)$.Dynamic Programming (DP): Applicable to problems exhibiting overlapping subproblems and optimal substructure. Instead of recomputing solutions to subproblems, DP stores previously computed results in a table (memoization for top-down approaches or tabular iteration for bottom-up approaches).Example: The standard matrix chain multiplication or 0/1 Knapsack problem reduce exponential time complexity $O(2^n)$ down to pseudo-polynomial or polynomial time $O(n \cdot W)$ by sacrificing space complexity.Greedy Paradigm: Makes the locally optimal choice at each decision point without reconsidering previous decisions.
